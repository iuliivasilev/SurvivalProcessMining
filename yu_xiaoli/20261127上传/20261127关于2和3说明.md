We used the full KDD Cup 2015 training log, which contains 8,157,277 events. 
We focused on the five courses with the most enrollments. Of their 47,706 enrollments, 43,383 had at least two learning actions after we removed page_close events. We left out the source field because it only tells us whether the browser or server recorded an event.

How we compared the records
For each enrollment, we counted individual actions and pairs of consecutive actions, then converted the counts to relative frequencies. This let us compare short and long records.

We tried two approaches. First, we compared each record with the average record for each of our four synthetic patterns using cosine similarity. We used softmax to turn the four scores into weights that add up to one. The pattern with the highest weight became the label for that enrollment.

Second, we used k-means to group similar KDD records into four clusters. Only after the groups had formed did we name each one after the synthetic pattern closest to its center.

Explorer was the closest direct match for 35,842 of the 43,383 enrollments. When we grouped similar records first, browser was the largest group. The two methods gave the same label to only 39% of enrollments. Most records in these five courses resembled our explorer example in the direct comparison, but KDD has no profile labels to tell us which type each student actually belonged to.
